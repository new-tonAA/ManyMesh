## Candidate paper spine

**Research question:** Can offline appearance optimization improve the rendered quality of a constrained triangle-mesh cluster LOD DAG after antialiasing or upscaling, while respecting shared boundaries, legal mixed cuts, and transitions?

Zhang and Chen's 2013 massive-mesh system already combines a coarse cluster hierarchy with a per-cluster progressive vertex hierarchy for GPU-parallel view-dependent refinement. Generic dual-hierarchy construction and fine-grained within-cluster refinement are therefore established prior art. Their paper optimizes projected geometric error and rendering efficiency, not image quality after AA or upscaling; this scope difference is not by itself a novelty claim. See the [direct mesh-hierarchy precedent](MANYMESH_UPSCALER_AWARE_DAG_RESEARCH.md#additional-direct-mesh-hierarchy-precedent).

This is a hypothesis, not a contribution claim. Full-text review confirms that Hasselgren et al. already optimize appearance against image-space references at chosen output scales and use MSAA during training to improve silhouette/visibility gradients. T²-Splat's full arXiv preprint combines adaptive triangle topology, differentiable image supervision, and scale-adaptive residual texture filtering. V3DG (SIGGRAPH 2025) already performs differentiable local fitting at each cluster-LOD level, then selects clusters by screen-space footprint; its 3D Gaussian representation and tree differ from a constrained triangle-mesh DAG, but the broader combination of hierarchical cluster LOD and per-level differentiable fitting is established. Ladeuil et al.'s HLOD ASAP abstract and publisher-provided first-page preview describe a binary hierarchy with internal and neighbor-paired boundary Progressive Meshes, allowing cluster interfaces to simplify while preserving consistent boundaries across a view-dependent cut. It explicitly identifies locked boundaries as an under-simplification failure. Quick-VDR (Yoon et al., IEEE Visualization 2004) already combines a clustered hierarchy of progressive meshes with dependency-constrained, crack-free view-dependent cuts. Its full paper says boundary-adjacent collapses are usually constrained, while cluster dependencies selectively permit boundary simplification by forcing linked clusters to refine together; this can add conservative triangles or cause popping. Rui Li's CVMP 2023 view-dependent HLOD abstract also describes child-parent vertex correspondence and interpolation to suppress cracks and popping during viewpoint-driven level selection. The ACM full text was inaccessible in this review, so its construction and quantitative comparison remain unverified. HLOD ASAP is the closer available precedent for progressive simplification of cluster boundaries. Thus shared boundaries, mixed cuts, hierarchy-aware fitting, and AA-aware scale selection are not novelty claims by themselves; geomorphing or transition stability alone is also not novel, and a locked-boundary prototype can only serve as a placement-isolation control. Other prior work covers learned mesh topology reduction, local refinement, differentiable image-guided adaptive LOD with watertightness constraints, joint mesh/appearance and mesh/shader refinement, foveated fixed-budget mesh selection, jointly optimized hierarchical 3DGS, perceptual transitions, and Nanite's subpixel-error/TAA strategy. The narrow possible distinction is a concrete algorithmic improvement on topology-distinct triangle-mesh DAG levels for measured post-reconstruction quality, with a benefit over progressive-boundary HLOD and real AA/upscalers at matched complete cost. This distinction has substantial novelty risk. The HLOD ASAP full text and quantitative results remain unavailable for verification; the [prior-art review](MANYMESH_UPSCALER_AWARE_DAG_RESEARCH.md) records review depth and unresolved comparisons. Novelty is not established.

An initial patent screen also found HTC's EP and US publications on selecting an optimal rendering/streaming tree from node quality, view visibility, and a triangle budget. The EP application is listed as pending in the publication record. These are relevant to hierarchy selection and resource allocation, but the inspected published claims do not establish post-AA image-driven geometry optimization. The screen is non-exhaustive and is not a patent-law analysis; see the [prior-art record](MANYMESH_UPSCALER_AWARE_DAG_RESEARCH.md).

Do not lead with mobile rendering, neural reconstruction, perceptual quality, temporal reconstruction, or compression. The repo has no physical-phone measurements or user study; existing temporal and rate-distortion screens do not establish a repeatable benefit that supports those claims. A desktop result may motivate a mobile study; it cannot establish one.

Quality metrics are necessary measurements, not a universal perceptual verdict. A 2026 VR study of dynamic meshes found only modest MOS correlation for PSNR, SSIM, MS-SSIM, Chamfer, and other common metrics in its protocol. Its QoE predictor scored highly under a random stimulus split, but that split mixed stimuli from the same eight meshes across train and test; cross-asset generalization was not established. A separate Web3D 2026 paper applies QoE prediction to LOD allocation under a GPU-memory budget, so perceptual/resource-aware LOD selection alone is not a contribution. The first study is available in full; the second is represented here only by its ACM record and indexed excerpts. See the [quality and perceptual-LOD prior-art review](MANYMESH_UPSCALER_AWARE_DAG_RESEARCH.md#direct-prior-art-on-subjective-quality-and-perceptual-lod).

## Image-quality evaluation contract

Use a metric panel because no single accepted metric covers geometric fidelity, raster appearance, temporal artifacts, and human preference. Report each metric separately; do not average unlike metrics into an invented universal quality score.

| Error family | Required measurements | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Rendered image | LDR-FLIP, PSNR, and SSIM against the same high-quality reference; also report silhouette coverage IoU/XOR and the fraction of squared error inside/outside the silhouette band. | FLIP is a perceptual-motivated image metric, not proof of user preference. PSNR/SSIM remain useful diagnostics and are not perceptual verdicts. Freeze exposure, tone mapping, materials, lighting, resolution, camera, AA, and reconstruction settings. |
| Surface geometry | Symmetric sampled surface distance (Chamfer), a high-percentile or Hausdorff-style distance, and normal deviation, with units and sampling density stated. | These diagnose surface and shading changes but do not predict final rendered quality on their own. |
| Motion and LOD transitions | CGVQM on fixed camera paths plus frame-to-frame image-difference/flicker traces and transition-local silhouette error. Release the sequences and report the metric implementation/version. | A temporal metric is evidence only for the tested content and path; do not call it perceptual without validation against ratings. |
| Human preference | A preregistered paired-comparison study (2AFC) on a held-out subset if the paper uses “perceptual,” “less noticeable,” or similar claims. | Report participant count, stimuli, randomization, exclusions, effect size, and uncertainty. An objective-metric win alone does not pass this gate. |

Treat the **asset** as the independent unit for generalization. Keep every view, camera-path frame, and LOD from a model in the same train, validation, or test split. Use development assets for tuning, freeze the method and controls, and evaluate the final test assets once. Report per-asset results and paired asset-bootstrap 95% intervals; do not count views or frames from one model as independent samples. Existing reused assets are development evidence, not a clean held-out set.

Run two distinct comparisons. First match actual final face count (or another explicitly frozen geometry budget) to isolate quality. Then match complete measured GPU frame time, including cut selection, geometry submission, AA/upscaling, and synchronization, to answer the practical tradeoff. Use the same scene, output resolution, camera path, materials, and reference for the candidate and controls. Report preparation/build time, sidecar or asset bytes, load/decode time, peak memory, selector CPU time, and per-frame GPU time separately; no omitted cost may be presented as free.

## Experiment order

Run low-cost falsification tests before implementing a differentiable hierarchy optimizer. This order is a screening strategy, not a novelty claim. The first selector screen has now run and failed its budget-feasibility gate.

1. **Contour-aware budgeted cut (Idea 3): closed under the current implementation.** The executable was built and run on 12 repository OBJ fixtures at requested ratios 0.3, 0.5, and 0.7. Across the 108 exported candidates, no cut had both the same face count and different geometry from the uniform cut; at 0.7, only two assets changed geometry with `k`, and those candidates missed the uniform face count. No image-quality metrics were scored. This is a budget-feasibility failure, not a visual-quality result. See the [screen report](tools/mmgeo/CONTOUR_SELECTOR_BUDGET_SCREEN_20261123.md) and [raw receipt](tools/mmgeo/contour_budget_screen_20261123.json). Do not tune or present this selector as a contribution.
2. **Scale-matched differentiable DAG optimization (Ideas 1 + A/B): not cleared by the current evidence gate.** The candidate question remains offline optimization of DAG geometry for the image after jittered accumulation/resolve and AA/upscaling, with transition stability and legal boundary behavior. It has not been tested and has substantial prior art. The plan's cheap differentiable-geometry screen is the matched-face Hasselgren/nvdiffmodeling placement baseline on hash-pinned, licensed assets with held-out views and AA/no-AA conditions. This host has CPU-only PyTorch, no CUDA runtime, and no in-repository Dragon asset, so that screen cannot run here. Do not start the custom hierarchy optimizer until compute and clean inputs are secured and the baseline shows a repeatable held-out signal. Compare against current placement, QEM, meshoptimizer, neural simplification where reproducible, progressive-boundary HLOD, and real reconstruction pipelines; account for full preparation and frame cost.
3. **Geomorphing and temporal stability (Idea 4).** Only if moving-camera captures show a material LOD-transition artifact after step 2, compare parent-child position blending with the current transition and any frozen temporal baseline. Measure transition-local error, a validated sequence metric, added vertex bandwidth/bytes, and full frame cost. Geomorphing and transition-aware LOD have prior art; this is an artifact-control study, not a standalone novelty claim.

Do not describe any step as “perceptual” without a user study or a predictor validated against ratings on asset-held-out data. Do not claim mobile benefit until the same frozen comparison runs on a physical phone. The [prior-art review](MANYMESH_UPSCALER_AWARE_DAG_RESEARCH.md) and [next-experiment gate](MANYMESH_NEXT_EXPERIMENT_GATE.md) record existing negative screens and unresolved infrastructure constraints.

## Evidence already available

- At 38% face retention on a fixed Monkey view, 4× MSAA gained **1.43 dB PSNR** and the tiny CNN gained **0.14 dB**. The test was a 960 × 720, grayscale, no-shadow desktop render with AA-matched references; the legacy report did not record the GPU. This makes equal-cost AA a required baseline and the pixel CNN an unsuitable headline. [MSAA record](MANYMESH_HISTORY.md#equal-aa-controls-and-lower-budget-saturation) · [CNN record](tools/mmtrain/README.md#cross-asset-check)
- The often-cited **0.11% / 68.3%** silhouette-error result compares a 7,124-face cut with an 11,250-face DAG cut, not with the original 15,744-face source. It is a one-model, one-view diagnostic, not a general error law; the historical input's source/license provenance also needs a hash-level audit before publication use.
- A new original-source Monkey diagnostic used eight views and the full 15,744-face OBJ reference. Silhouette-error shares were **34.75%, 25.84%, and 25.88%** at 30%, 50%, and 70% requested retention. Exact directed-edge matching flagged every cut, but a retrospective one-to-one coordinate map matched all 168 oriented boundary edges within **0.52 float32 epsilon** at unit radius. Eligible exact-face QEM/meshoptimizer controls beat its image metrics at 30% and 50%, and meshoptimizer beat it at 70%. This repairs the reference choice but remains a one-asset diagnostic, not a held-out claim; preregister a geometric-boundary tolerance before confirmation evaluation. See the [report](tools/mmtrain/MONKEY_ORIGINAL_SOURCE_DIAGNOSTIC_20261123.md), [image receipt](tools/mmtrain/monkey_source_reference_diagnostic_20261123.json), and [boundary audit](tools/mmtrain/monkey_boundary_curve_audit_20261123.json).
- A multi-view attribution reanalysis on the reused Thingi10K development splits puts the split-mean silhouette-XOR share at **24–37%** across 50% and 70% face retention. For the stronger meshoptimizer outputs, the per-asset mean exceeded 50% on **1/11** train assets at 50% retention and **0/7** validation assets at 50%, **0/14** finite training outputs at 70%, and **0/8** validation assets at 70%. This flat-shaded 4× box-sampled diagnostic is not perceptual evidence, and the same assets occur at both budgets. It argues against a silhouette-only thesis; it does not answer what error remains after temporal reconstruction. ## Minimum paper evidence if every gate passes

1. **A precise algorithmic contribution.** Specify the variables and gradients, multi-level objective, boundary and parent/child constraints, transition regularizer, and convergence/failure behavior. Explain what is algorithmically new relative to Hasselgren et al., V3DG, appearance-preserving scene aggregation, neural cross-LOD prefiltering, Neural Mesh Simplification, Learned Localized Mesh Refinement (SIGGRAPH Asia 2026, adaptive learned upsampling of coarse input meshes), differentiable μ-mesh construction, LTM, automatic mesh/shader LOD, foveated fixed-budget mesh LOD, T²-Splat, HLoD, sampling-aware 3DGS LOD, view-dependent simplification (including Quick-VDR and Rui Li's 2023 parent-child interpolation HLOD), perceptual LOD, Nanite, and the 2026 clustered-HLOD method. Compare transition quality and complete cost against existing parent-child interpolation methods; do not claim crack-free transitions or geomorphing as new. Read full texts for close works currently represented only by abstracts or previews before making novelty claims.
2. **Fair geometry and reconstruction controls.** Include QEM, current ManyMesh placement, meshoptimizer cluster LOD, faithful nvdiffmodeling, and Neural Mesh Simplification where a fair replication is available, plus standard LOD with MSAA/SMAA and available upscalers. The fixed sweep recovered two legal exact-face pairs against stock, but both lost on every reported image metric and the asset sample is only two. Do not score or silently discard invalid cuts as quality evidence. Compare the hierarchy and constraints against HLOD ASAP, Rui Li's parent-child interpolation HLOD, Quick-VDR, and relevant adaptive μ-mesh work, documenting representation differences and source-verification limits. Match face budgets and separately match complete frame time; do not compare only image quality at different cost.
3. **Quality and temporal reporting.** Report LDR-FLIP, silhouette/coverage error, PSNR, SSIM, geometric surface/normal error, topology and boundary checks, and validated temporal quality on moving camera paths. A frame-difference statistic alone is not a perceptual metric. Treat objective metrics as complementary unless they are validated against ratings for the tested content and viewing conditions. If claiming perceived improvement, run a predeclared, adequately powered user study (for example, paired comparison or DSIS with randomized order) or validate a perceptual predictor against ratings. Report metric-to-rating correlation and uncertainty; do not reuse a QoE model's random-stimulus split as evidence of generalization to unseen assets.
4. **Scale, uncertainty, and provenance.** Use a fresh licensed, category-balanced held-out asset set with at least 20 assets across five or more predeclared shape categories, multiple held-out views and camera paths, paired asset-level uncertainty intervals, and predeclared effect margins/exclusions. Split all training, validation, and test data by source asset: no views, LODs, frames, or camera paths from one mesh may leak across partitions. The existing 32-asset corpus has already influenced prior work and cannot be presumed to provide a clean test split. Capture the exact project Git commit; if the run uses a dirty tree, archive and hash the patch; also record the upstream revision, experiment script and executable hashes, per-asset license/credit, geometry and view hashes, importer version, eligibility checks, exclusions, environment, and frozen split before scoring. Link every paper number to that run script and raw receipt. The older Monkey silhouette/AA and draw-range figures currently lack verified generating commits and must be reproduced or excluded. The Khronos asset catalog is a promising source but is rich in feature-test models and does not yet meet this gate; see the [asset screening protocol](MANYMESH_CONFIRMATION_ASSET_SCREEN.md) and [claim provenance audit](MANYMESH_CLAIM_EVIDENCE.md).
5. **Complete cost.** Measure offline optimization time, peak memory, sidecar/model bytes, load/decode cost, selector cost, GPU time, and complete frame time. Add mobile power/thermal/device claims only after real supported phone measurements.
