# SIGGRAPH 2027 prior-art screen

Updated 2026-11-27. This is a bounded lead list, not a systematic review,
novelty opinion, or legal assessment. Sources were checked at their publisher,
author, project, or explicitly identified preview pages; only entries marked
**paper text inspected** were checked beyond an abstract or summary. Read the full closest papers,
their references, and citing work before making a novelty claim. Failure to find
a paper is not evidence that none exists.

| Choi et al., [LTM: Lightweight Textured Mesh Extraction and Refinement of Large Unbounded Scenes](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Choi_LTM_Lightweight_Textured_Mesh_Extraction_and_Refinement_of_Large_Unbounded_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf), CVPR 2024 | Extracts meshes from neural SDF scene reconstructions, applies vertex clustering and geometry-aware QSlim decimation, then uses nvdiffrast to optimize per-vertex deformation and a neural appearance field against multi-view image L1 loss. It regularizes with Laplacian smoothness, adjacent-face normal consistency, and a high-resolution normal target. During optimization it subdivides faces with large displacement and re-decimates; it reports PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, mesh/storage size, and 212 FPS with 115 MB GPU memory at 1080p in a browser on a 2020 MacBook Pro M1. | **Full paper text inspected** (Sections 3.2–4.2, Tables 1–5). This is direct prior art for image-guided optimization and topology refinement after mesh simplification, plus a laptop rendering result. It reconstructs captured unbounded scenes rather than simplifying an authored asset into a legal cluster-DAG, and does not optimize through a specified final MSAA/upscaler. The MacBook result is not a phone measurement. A cluster-DAG/post-reconstruction distinction remains a test question, not proof of novelty. |
| Hasselgren et al., [Appearance-Driven Automatic 3D Model Simplification](https://diglib.eg.org/items/82ec8df1-d62a-40f0-a007-90374b5140dc), EGSR 2021 | Differentiable image-space optimization jointly fits mesh vertex positions and materials; uses include appearance-preserving decimation and LOD. | Funkhouser and Séquin, [Adaptive Display Algorithm for Interactive Frame Rates During Visualization of Complex Virtual Environments](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr01/cs598b/papers/funkhouser93.pdf), SIGGRAPH 1993 | Selects one LOD and rendering algorithm per potentially visible object to maximize estimated image benefit under a target frame-time constraint. ### In-engine stochastic rasterizer gradients

Deliot, Heitz, and Belcour's [Transforming a Non-Differentiable Rasterizer
into a Differentiable One with Stochastic Gradient Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09758)
(PACMCGIT 2024) adds ID/UV buffers and two compute shaders to a conventional
rasterizer, then estimates per-pixel geometry and material gradients with
stochastic perturbations. The paper explicitly targets optimization for the
asset's final in-engine rendering and reports scenes with more than one million
parameters.

**Full arXiv v2 text inspected**, including method, mesh applications, results,
and limitations. This closes generic claims to in-engine image-driven geometry
optimization, per-pixel stochastic gradients, or avoiding a vendor-specific
differentiable renderer. The paper does not present simplification as its
contribution, a cluster-DAG constraint, or optimization through MSAA or a
temporal upscaler; the terms `MSAA` and `anti-alias` do not occur in its text.
It is a required fixed-topology optimizer baseline if the target rasterizer
can expose the needed per-pixel contributor IDs. These differences are test
dimensions, not proof of novelty.

### Mesh-quality constraints for vertex fitting

Blauth and Leithäuser, [Enforcing Mesh Quality Constraints in Shape
Optimization with a Gradient Projection Method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00006),
published in *Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering* (2025),
project a search direction onto active mesh-quality constraints, correct active
constraints after a step, and modify line search to prevent new violations.
They formulate minimum and maximum angle bounds for 2D triangular meshes and
solid-angle bounds for 3D tetrahedral cells, including treatment of fixed
boundaries. Their application is PDE-constrained shape optimization, not
stochastic raster image fitting or an authored surface-mesh cluster DAG.
Nevertheless, generic constraint-aware mesh fitting and feasible line search
are established; such an optimizer is a required numerical control, not a
novelty claim. Their discussion also identifies the practical failure mode
seen in the local Deliot-style replay: quality constraints can force small
steps and stall an unconstrained optimizer.

## Candidate gap and required test

The remaining experiment question is narrowly phrased as **whether offline
optimization of an existing triangle-mesh cluster hierarchy for quality after
a specified final antialiasing or upscaling pipeline yields a measurable gain
over applying the same operator to unoptimized geometry**. This is not an
established gap or novelty claim. Differentiable image-space geometry fitting,
image-guided adaptive per-triangle LOD, and changing mesh topology under
multi-view image losses with antialiasing-derived visibility gradients are
also established. In-engine per-pixel stochastic rasterizer gradients are
established too. HLOD, parent-child vertex relations, view-dependent
selection, crack-free cuts, and crack-/popping-free transitions are
established mechanisms too. See Deliot et al., Hasselgren et al., Dou et al.,
DMesh++, Quick-VDR, Li (2022, 2023), and HLOD ASAP.
“LOD for upscalers,” “silhouette aware,” “differentiable LOD,” “cluster
hierarchy optimization,” and “temporal stability” are not novelty claims by
themselves.

Before investing in a custom method:

1. Reproduce Hasselgren et al.'s fixed-topology mesh-placement baseline against
   QEM, ManyMesh placement, and meshoptimizer at identical face counts. Treat
   the μ-mesh paper's visual-guided per-face subdivision as a close adaptive-LOD
   control, while keeping its displacement representation and ray-tracing
   objective distinct from a standard-mesh raster comparison.
2. Verify the original Luebke–Erikson proceedings paper and obtain the full
   HLOD ASAP and Li CVMP 2023 papers. The related Li i3D 2023 poster paper has
   been inspected in full, but does not substitute for the CVMP paper. Inspect
   references and citing work for additional mesh-level predecessors. The
   Luebke–Erikson author dissertation and the complete Funkhouser–Séquin paper
   have been inspected for the claims above. Mason–Blake's corrected 1999
   report has also been read; the 1997 proceedings paper is represented here
   by its publisher abstract and the authors' correction.
3. If the matched-face baseline has a held-out signal, test whether the
   cluster-DAG constraints add a repeatable benefit after actual MSAA or an
   available upscaler, at both matched faces and matched complete frame time.
4. Treat any unlocated prior art as unresolved. Update this screen with full
   citations, exact sections compared, implementation differences, and negative
   results; do not turn a search gap into a novelty statement.
