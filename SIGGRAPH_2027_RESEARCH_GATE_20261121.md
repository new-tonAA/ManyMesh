# SIGGRAPH 2027 research gate (updated 2026-11-23)

## Status

The SIGGRAPH-level algorithm goal is **not achieved**. No candidate has both a defensible novelty boundary and repeatable quality/performance gains over strong baselines. Keep this as an active research goal; do not present the work as submission-ready.

## Current direction

The tested per-cluster polynomial linear-subspace decoder configurations at ranks
8, 16, and 30 are closed. The [same-face
meshoptimizer comparison](tools/mmtrain/CLUSTER_DECODER_MESHOPT_20261122.md) finds no
validation gain: fixed displacement is effectively zero, and local refinement loses
to meshoptimizer at the same final face count. No current candidate is submission-ready.
The path-loss hypothesis documented in earlier records is not validated either.

The latest falsification screens do not change that decision. The contour-risk
selector produced no equal-face, geometry-distinct cuts on 12 repository fixtures
and is closed in its tested form. An original-source Monkey diagnostic compared
three DAG cuts against exact-face QEM and meshoptimizer controls over eight views;
after a retrospective one-float32-ULP boundary rule, every cut was eligible, but
the controls still won on every reported image metric where an eligible control
existed. This is one reused asset, not held-out evidence, and the tolerance must
be frozen before any confirmation run. See the [selector screen](tools/mmgeo/CONTOUR_SELECTOR_BUDGET_SCREEN_20261123.md),
[Monkey report](tools/mmtrain/MONKEY_ORIGINAL_SOURCE_DIAGNOSTIC_20261123.md),
and [one-ULP receipt](tools/mmtrain/monkey_source_float32_boundary_diagnostic_20261123.json).

The separate differentiable-geometry baseline remains unrun: this host has no
CUDA runtime, and the local candidate assets do not yet form a clean licensed,
topology-valid confirmation pair. Do not treat the failed contour selector as a
test of that independent placement hypothesis, and do not start a custom DAG
optimizer without a runnable strong baseline. The current detailed protocol and

## Evidence reviewed

- A focused primary-source audit confirms high novelty risk for the decoder branch. Hasselgren et al. (EGSR 2021) already optimize mesh and shading from differentiable image-space loss for decimation and LOD. NeCGS (ICCV 2025) compresses geometry sets including 1,000 Thingi10K models with a shared quantization-aware auto-decoder, per-model embedded features, entropy coding, and TSDF-Def-to-mesh reconstruction; it reports rate-distortion, surface metrics, and decompression time. Neural Progressive Meshes (SIGGRAPH 2023) use a shared subdivision decoder plus per-face progressive residual features. Learned Localized Mesh Refinement is a SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 paper deferred to present at SIGGRAPH 2027 and covers local learned topology/geometry refinement with selected-region and view-dependent use. Therefore neither changing to render loss, per-asset latents, nor generic local learned refinement alone distinguishes ManyMesh. The NeCGS implementation is public but has not been reproduced here. See the [updated comparison and exact limits](MANYMESH_CLUSTER_DECODER_STUDY.md#prior-art-comparison).
- The 2026-11-22 [same-face meshoptimizer screen](tools/mmtrain/CLUSTER_DECODER_MESHOPT_20261122.md) compares image and sampled surface quality across five paired validation assets. Meshoptimizer improves PSNR by 9.72 dB over the earlier QEM result (95% asset-bootstrap interval [+6.48, +12.55]). The retrained rank-8 fixed decoder leaves the meshoptimizer output unchanged. At equal final face count, local refinement loses 2.02 dB on four paired validation assets (95% interval [-2.25, -1.78]) and also loses on SSIM, FLIP, LPIPS, and silhouette. These close the tested decoder configurations; the test split was not evaluated.
- The [rank 8/16/30 capacity screen](tools/mmtrain/CLUSTER_DECODER_RANK_SWEEP_20261122.md) tested the maximum rank of the current polynomial basis on the same train/validation split. All three fixed-topology ranks lose to QEM on the paired validation image metrics; ranks 16 and 30 add bytes, and rank 30 fails to decode two of five prepared validation meshes. This closes that linear control family, not learned decoding generally. The test split remains unused.
- A [validation-only latent-scale probe](tools/mmtrain/CLUSTER_DECODER_SCALE_PROBE_20261122.md) tests alpha 0, .25, .5, .75, and 1 for the frozen rank-8 residual on five prepared assets. On the four assets that decode at nonzero scales, every nonzero scale loses to the alpha-0 identity across PSNR, SSIM, FLIP, and LPIPS; paired 95% asset-bootstrap intervals exclude zero in the worse direction. The fifth asset rejects all nonzero scales under the decoder's topology/offset checks. This argues against a simple overlarge-residual explanation, not against learned decoding generally. Per-asset scale selection is an oracle and the test split remains unused; see the [compact receipt](tools/mmtrain/cluster_decoder_scale_oracle_20261122.json).
- A separate [70%-retention QEM/meshoptimizer sweep](tools/mmtrain/CLUSTER_DECODER_BASELINE_70PCT_VALIDATION_20261122.md) evaluates all eight validation assets. Mean PSNR change is +2.00 dB with a 95% asset-bootstrap interval [-0.39, +4.62]; SSIM improves by +0.00222 [+0.00037, +0.00566], while FLIP, silhouette, and mean Chamfer intervals include zero. Meshoptimizer passes topology and boundary checks on 7/8 outputs; the rejected output remains included in the raw image-metric screen and is separately counted as a geometry failure. This is a baseline comparison, not a decoder result.
- An [unmodified upstream meshoptimizer v1.2 cluster LOD baseline](tools/mmtrain/UPSTREAM_CLUSTERLOD_SCREEN_20261122.md) passed the complete-cut edge and boundary checks at all three exported settings on eight assets. At depth 1 it improves mean PSNR by 3.60 dB and mean LDR-FLIP by 0.000976 over the six valid, exact-face QEM pairs. A historical ManyMesh binary has five whole-cut topology failures at both depth budgets, despite enabling the local gate. The binary hash and per-asset face counts are recorded.
- A [serialized DAG audit and constrained cut solve](tools/mmgeo/HISTORICAL_CUT_TOPOLOGY_AUDIT_20261122.md) identifies 676 nonmanifold edges introduced across multiple nodes in the historical cuts. It recovers edge-valid cuts for five of the eight assets at the first-depth cap, up from two. All five have higher LDR-FLIP than the upstream cut. Current C++ source compiled, but execution of the fresh binary was blocked by Windows application control.
- [Rendered reranking of 44 feasible saved cuts](tools/mmgeo/SAVED_CUT_VIEW_RERANK_20261122.md) improves held-out LDR-FLIP over geometric sorting on two of five feasible assets. All five still lose to the upstream cut at the same cap, including each pool's best held-out result. Close this formulation.
- A [Draco and meshoptimizer codec sweep](tools/mmtrain/GEOMETRY_CODEC_RATE_DISTORTION_20261122.md) measures standalone geometry bytes and decoded render quality on the eight validation assets. Full meshes quantized at 20 bits pass topology checks on 8/8 assets. Draco's mean byte saving is 29.8% (95% paired asset-bootstrap interval [26.8%, 32.6%]) at identical LDR-FLIP. The measurements cover geometry streams and warm WASM decode. Full asset size, cluster access, and renderer integration require separate evidence.
- A [64-origin quantization search](tools/mmtrain/QUANTIZATION_GRID_ORIGIN_20261122.md) uses the existing Draco position bitstream with the same precision and grid spacing as a fixed-origin control. On eight validation assets, two pass the geometry checks at a lower tested precision and save bytes, but both increase LDR-FLIP. The fixed-precision joint improvements save at most three bytes. Close this formulation.
- The same screen was run on the 16 training assets as a separate development diagnostic. One QEM output was rejected; only 10/15 paired meshoptimizer outputs pass strict topology and boundary checks. Metrics favor meshoptimizer on several image measures, but surface results are mixed. This split is not independent evidence and is not combined with validation statistics; see the [training report](tools/mmtrain/CLUSTER_DECODER_BASELINE_70PCT_TRAIN_20261122.md) and [receipt](tools/mmtrain/cluster_decoder_qem_meshopt_train_70pct_20261122.json).
- An offscreen Iris Xe comparison measures native rendering, 4x MSAA, and half-resolution reconstruction on three 16-instance scenes at 1080p. The 1.74M-triangle ToyCar cut plus MSAA reaches 30.914 dB / 0.981750 SSIM at 0.956 ms median GPU time; full-source native rendering is 32.685 dB / 0.990573 at 3.014 ms. Full-source linear 2x reconstruction reaches 29.521 dB / 0.976297 at 3.142 ms. GlassVaseFlowers favors full-source upscaling. The mixed, fixed-camera result does not measure a full Filament frame or mobile device; see the [1080p GPU report](tools/mmtrain/CLUSTER_DECODER_GPU_MSAA_1080_BASELINE_20261122.md) and [720p repeat](tools/mmtrain/CLUSTER_DECODER_GPU_MSAA_BASELINE_20261122.md).
- A 13-asset dynamic LOD screen first found +0.258 dB grayscale PSNR under per-asset calibration, but shared-profile leave-one-asset-out evaluation reduced the mean change to +0.083 dB (95% asset-bootstrap CI [-0.019, +0.190]); SSIM is inconclusive and silhouette IoU declines (95% CI [-0.000351, -0.000085]). The assets/component pairs are reused development data, calibration time is excluded, and the workload is isolated and flat-shaded. Benefit/cost LOD allocation and hysteresis have prior art. Close this formulation; it does not establish a novel method or mobile-performance claim. See the [per-asset protocol](tools/mmtrain/DYNAMIC_LOD_13ASSET_20261122.md) and [leave-one-asset-out results](tools/mmtrain/DYNAMIC_LOD_LOAO_13ASSET_20261122.md).
- The original dynamic LOD runs contain framewise PSNR but no stored sequences. A later opt-in capture writes lossless FFV1 with exact pixel round-trip verification. A CGVQM-5 screen on two held-out paths across 13 reused Khronos assets found path-averaged selected-profile minus area mean -0.0590 (95% asset-bootstrap CI [-0.1476, +0.0424]); 4 assets improved and 9 regressed. The interval crosses zero, and the screen uses a custom OpenCV FFmpeg decode adapter, not upstream PyAV. See the [CGVQM report](tools/mmmetrics/CGVQM_DYNAMIC_LOD_SCREEN_20261122.md) and [capture/metric protocol](tools/mmmetrics/TEMPORAL_QUALITY_PROTOCOL.md).
- A separate 13-asset, four-path screen captures sequences and measures a custom foreground temporal finite-difference error. The shared profile improves its mean by 0.000942 (95% asset-bootstrap CI [0.000488, 0.001434] in the improvement direction) and reduces switch-bearing transitions by 2.08 per 48-frame path. Across paired fixed-camera renders, the per-asset calibrated component cut plus 4x MSAA improves mean PSNR by 5.66 dB and SSIM by 0.01689 over full-source linear 2x reconstruction, but is faster on only 4/13 assets; only 4/13 improve both quality metrics and GPU time. This calibrated cut is not a deployable general policy, and the curated asset set does not establish population generalization. On 13 full-source meshes SMAA is faster on 7 and improves LDR-FLIP on none. A two-pixel reference copy around the coarse silhouette moves three additional assets ahead of the best controls at the existing cut's GPU time. - The [mixed-scene baseline](tools/mmtrain/MIXED_SCENE_BASELINES_20261122.md) covers three rigid, grayscale scenes with 1.65–2.15 million submitted source triangles, 35–48 instances and three views per scene. Those stock cuts retain more faces, use different index streams, and introduce nonmanifold edges on three source-clean meshes. It is faster than dense raster sampling on some candidate pools, but not others; in several cases the cheaper raster already chooses an effectively tied candidate. The higher-resolution Pirate sequence tied QEM on coverage, was 1.57% worse on flat-normal loss, and cost 16.2x as much CPU time. The cache does not turn that branch into a quality-cost win. See the [paired audit](tools/mmgeo/PATH_LOSS_CACHE_MONKEY_4PCT_20261122.json) and [path-loss frontier record](tools/mmgeo/PATH_LOSS_POOL_FRONTIER_20261121.md).
- A new rendered audit evaluates the continuous-pan path-loss reranker against same-face QEM on four development assets and eight orbit views per asset. Mean PSNR changes by +0.224 dB (95% paired asset-bootstrap CI [-0.182, +0.782]); SSIM and LDR-FLIP intervals also cross zero, and construction takes 4.5–15.1x the QEM time. The screen adds direct image evidence but does not pass the gate. See the [report](tools/mmgeo/PATH_LOSS_RENDER_QUALITY_20261122.md) and [render receipt](tools/mmgeo/PATH_LOSS_RENDER_RECEIPT_20261122.json).
- The on-demand DAG reader bounds cache residency but does not incrementally build the hierarchy. An incremental, strict-gate builder under a hard memory cap remains an engineering/research question, but generic out-of-core simplification and streamed LOD are established prior art. See [memory screen](tools/mmgeo/ON_DEMAND_DAG_BUILD_MEMORY_SCREEN_20261118.md).
- Birkholz's WSCG 2006 method builds an external-memory continuous LOD hierarchy with an adjustable memory limit. Derzapf et al. (VMV 2010) store view-dependent progressive split/collapse operations in file-backed spatial nodes and stream them under a per-frame transfer cap. Together they preempt generic bounded-memory construction, progressive partial availability, and capped streaming as novelty claims. Any strict-gate builder must compare against both construction and runtime-streaming systems. See the [prior-art boundary and falsifiable screen](tools/mmgeo/ON_DEMAND_DAG_BUILD_MEMORY_SCREEN_20261118.md#prior-art-boundary).
- Recent work further narrows the space: [crack-free progressive HLOD boundaries (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.70380), [GPU out-of-core HLOD rendering (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3731186), [localized learned mesh refinement (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31946), and [split-and-rewire learned mesh generation (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38985) overlap with the current boundary, memory, and decoder hypotheses. Their full methods and code still need direct inspection before any novelty claim.
- The filtered-boundary branch also has direct neighbors: EdgeGrad (ECCV 2024) computes raster-loss gradients at mesh visibility edges and intersections, and DiffSoup (CVPR 2026) reconstructs a budgeted triangle soup from multiview images. Neither directly supplies a legal simplification sequence for an authored connected mesh, but antialiasing-aware loss, edge gradients, and image-driven triangle budgets are not sufficient novelty claims. See the [updated boundary audit](MANYMESH_FILTER_AWARE_SIMPLIFICATION.md#2026-11-22-prior-art-update).

## Novelty boundary

Generic view-dependent simplification, continuous viewcell error bounds, and image-driven mesh simplification are established. For example, [Eisemann and Décoret (2007)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2007.01013.x) give exact error bounds over polygonal viewcells, while image-driven simplification predates this project. Therefore neither “continuous camera path,” “exact visibility,” nor “image-space mesh simplification” is a sufficient novelty claim.

No current method has a validated novelty boundary. Any new candidate requires a
specific comparison against established view-dependent simplification, mesh LOD,
image-driven simplification, and learned geometry methods. The decoder protocol
[MANYMESH_CLUSTER_DECODER_STUDY.md](MANYMESH_CLUSTER_DECODER_STUDY.md) records its
prior-art neighbors and evidence gate.

## Next gate

Do not promote the tested decoder or path-loss variants. Before investing in a new mechanism, complete a focused prior-art comparison and state a falsifiable contribution. The next experiment must compare a frozen implementation against strong baselines on multiple independent assets and held-out views. The gate is:

The reviewer-suggested silhouette-reconstruction direction is not novel by itself:
silhouette maps/clipping, silhouette-sensitive perceptual simplification, and
SMAA's image-space edge reconstruction are established. A 50%-retention screen
found binary-coverage XOR pixels explain 27%-37% of squared image error on average
across the tested train/validation splits, versus 68.3% in the historical single
Monkey view. Any follow-up must establish a narrower target and beat strong edge
baselines at matched quality and complete cost. See the [attribution report](tools/mmtrain/SILHOUETTE_ERROR_ATTRIBUTION_20261122.md)
and [expanded prior-art table](MANYMESH_CLUSTER_DECODER_STUDY.md#prior-art-comparison).

1. Fix assets, splits, camera trajectories, renderer, image/surface metrics, and resource budget before scoring.
2. Include QEM/CornerSimplifier, meshoptimizer, and the closest published system for the chosen problem at matched quality or resource cost.
3. Report complete pipeline wall time, peak process working set, bytes, topology failures, and per-asset paired uncertainty; do not count views as independent assets.
4. Require a repeatable quality-cost Pareto improvement on held-out assets. If the result depends on one asset, a weak comparator, or an unmeasured resource assumption, close the branch.
5. Only after passing this gate, evaluate on a fresh licensed confirmation set and consider production integration.

For a decoder follow-up, do not promote multi-view render-loss fitting, per-asset
latents, or localized refinement by themselves: image-space simplification, set
compression with shared decoder weights, progressive mesh residuals, and local
learned refinement are established. First define and test a distinction from
NeCGS, Neural Progressive Meshes, localized learned refinement, and meshoptimizer
at matched rate-distortion and complete asset cost; otherwise close this branch.
The updated [prior-art comparison](MANYMESH_CLUSTER_DECODER_STUDY.md#prior-art-comparison)
records the overlap and its current evidence limits.

The current goal remains active. No tested candidate passes the evidence and novelty gates, and this status document is not a submission-ready paper.
